### `scripts/score_ontology.py`

Direct OWL/TTL artifact scorer. Companion to `aegir-verify.py`
(which scores compositions over the catalog). Computes each
R-component from DeepOnto-derived properties:

- **R_A** — 1.0 iff DeepOnto loads, else 0.0 (hard gate).
- **R_B** — `min(|asserted_complex| / τ_B, 1)`.
- **R_C** — `mean_verbal_length / L_target` clipped, computed
  over named-class subsumption + equivalence + complex-
  expression verbalizations.
- **R_D** — Hungarian-aligned cosine of T_V (BERT-equivalent
  fit on the ontology's verbalizations) vs the cached T_I,
  normalized against `null_stats`.

### `scripts/tune_verifier_weights.py`

Scores the entire labeled test set, sweeps `{a, b, c}` over the
`a + b + c = 1` simplex (step 0.05, 231 grid points), reports
AUC + separation, identifies optimal weights subject to the
gate thresholds.

### `Justfile` recipe `just c1-validate`

End-to-end: regenerate test set + score + sweep + report.

## Sweep findings

107 weight triples (out of 231 grid points) satisfy both
threshold gates. Notable observations from the sweep:

- **AUC plateau at 0.9956**: many `{a, b, c}` triples achieve
  identical AUC because once R_B alone perfectly orders good
  vs bad in the upper region of the joint distribution, weight
  redistribution within the AUC-preserving cone changes
  separation but not ranking.
- **Maximum AUC** at `{0.95, 0.05, 0.00}` (R_B-only). Rejected
  in favor of multi-component balance.
- **Maximum separation under AUC ≥ 0.95 and c ≥ 0.10**:
  `{0.85, 0.05, 0.10}` at sep = 0.5186. Also rejected — too
  R_B-dominated.
- **Locked at `{0.50, 0.05, 0.45}`** — substantial R_D
  contribution preserved, separation 0.3388 (above threshold),
  AUC 0.9956 (plateau-max).

## Methodological observations

**R_C as currently defined is a weak discriminator** on this
test set. Bad ontologies that have many axioms (e.g.,
`bad_no_labels.ttl`, `bad_no_complex.ttl`,
`bad_random_gibberish.ttl`) produce high R_C because their
verbalizations have non-zero length, even though the content
is meaningless. Future iterations may strengthen R_C by:
- Penalizing trivial verbalizations (e.g., "X is a Y"
  patterns with very short labels).
- Normalizing by axiom complexity rather than raw length.
- Incorporating label quality (e.g., real English vs gibberish)
  via a perplexity-style filter.

**R_D's size-dependence** affects most test-set ontologies. The
null distribution was computed against 24-template random
compositions; test ontologies have 8–17 named classes and
2–5 complex axioms, producing 5–30 verbalizations — fewer
than the null's typical 24. Compositions matching null size
(e.g., the full SDG catalog at ~150 templates) will normalize
correctly. For C1 evaluation, the size-dependence still allows
useful discrimination (`good_database_metadata.ttl` raw
alignment was high enough to saturate R_D=1.0).

**R_B is the load-bearing component** of the verifier on this
test set. The complex-class density distinction between
intentionally-authored ontologies (5+ complex axioms) and
empty/trivial ones (0 complex) is sharp. This is by design:
the brief specifies R_B as "complex-class density" because
genuine ontologies require complex structure.
