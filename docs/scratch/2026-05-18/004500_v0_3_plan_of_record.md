# Aegir v0.3 — Plan of record (publishable artifact lineup)

**Date:** 2026-05-18 00:45 UTC
**Status:** draft for tomorrow's review

## Thesis (re-stated)

Byte-level H-Net/RWKV trained on **deterministically-verifiable, ontology-grounded synthetic textbook content** can achieve performance comparable to specialized table-models (TAPAS/TAPEX/TableFormer) on column type annotation, while producing a corpus that is *useful as a corpus* (separable, publishable, attribution-clean) — not just consumed in training and forgotten.

The corpus is the product, not just the training input.

### Track B — The chapter corpus

**4. Chapter corpus (5K-10K chapters)**
- Generated by `scripts/generate_chapter.py` with the 60/40 GLM/Grok mix
- Format: parquet (chapter_id, family, template_ids, style_topic_ids, response_text, ablation, model, latency_ms) + per-chapter .md
- Storage: PyIceberg + filesystem warehouse on /raid
- Pre-alpha tonight: ablation_v1 (600 chapters across 3 arms) is generating in background
- Published as: HuggingFace dataset card + Iceberg-table-on-S3 mirror

**5. CoT trace corpus (the separable trace artifact)**
- The reasoning_content from both GLM-4.7 and Grok-4.3, persisted in HX exchange table alongside the chapter response
- This is the *novel* publishable artifact — table-grounded synthesis reasoning traces, ontology-anchored
- Format: parquet (exchange_id, model, request_messages, response_reasoning, response_content, source_context)
- Use case: train smaller models on the reasoning traces, study the CoT-quality / corpus-quality relationship
- Published as: HuggingFace dataset with attribution and license

**6. Per-arm ablation evidence**
- The 3-arm ablation results (full / no-ontology / no-schema)
- Demonstrates: ontology IS load-bearing for ontology-grounded downstream tasks, but trades topic-distribution alignment for structural grounding
- Memos in `docs/scratch/2026-05-18/`: ablation pilot, structural alignment
- Published as: paper-section with the four measurement axes (topic correspondence, R_axiom, structural alignment, downstream LM loss)

### Track C — Trained models and downstream eval

**7. Aegir-tiny ablation checkpoints (indicative)**
- Three checkpoints, one per ablation arm, trained on identical hyperparameters
- Infrastructure ready: `scripts/chapters_to_byte_parquet.py` + `scripts/train_ablation_all.sh`
- Expected signal: tiny corpus (1M tokens) under-Chinchilla, differential will be small. Useful as "does ANY signal exist" check before scaling.
- Published as: checkpoints + tensorboard logs on HuggingFace

**8. Aegir-small downstream-task checkpoint (target)**
- Trained on the full (e.g. 5K chapter / 25M token) corpus
- Eval target: not SOTAB-CTA (we've shown that's the wrong target — see structural-alignment memo); instead a CPA-style eval against our own 540-template axioms ("which slot-type does this column header occupy in the ontology")
- Comparison baseline: TAPEX, TableFormer, plain RWKV-7 of equivalent params
- Published as: weights + eval script + leaderboard entry

**9. Methodology / paper / arxiv preprint**
- Frame: "Ontology-grounded synthetic pretraining for byte-level table-aware language models"
- Sections: thesis, ablation design, coverage methodology, corpus characterization, downstream eval, comparison to TAPAS lineage
- Co-published: arxiv + HF blog post + GitHub release v0.3.0

## Open decisions for tomorrow's discussion

**D1.** Is SOTAB-CTA the right eval target? Structural alignment shows our ontology vocabulary diverges from Schema.org. Two options:
- (a) accept the v0.2 ROADMAP target (SOTAB-CTA) and ship a no-ontology-arm checkpoint
- (b) re-target to ontology-grounded CPA, ship the full-pipeline checkpoint, frame the SOTAB-CTA divergence as evidence the benchmark is misaligned with our thesis

**D2.** What corpus mix do we ship at scale?
- 60/40 GLM/Grok (current default)
- Per-family weighting (some families show more LIMS-friendliness than others)
- The cost shape at 5K chapters: ~$45 at 60/40, ~$15 at GLM-only, ~$150 at Grok-only

**D3.** Per-arm at scale or single-corpus at scale?
- The structural alignment finding suggests "no-ontology" produces a structurally different corpus, not just lower-quality one
- We could ship both full AND no-ontology corpora as separate releases, each a different proposition
- This is more publishable but doubles eval cost

**D4.** Where do we put the budget ceiling tonight?
- Ablation v1 (600 chapters, ~$3) — already running
- A 5K-chapter scale pass at 60/40 mix would be ~$45 and 1 day wall
- Worth committing tonight to land before the broader v0.3 release window?

**D5.** What CTA/CPA dev set do we hold out?
- Our own 540-template axioms can be used as a CPA-style eval (column→slot_type)
- We need a held-out chapter set with HIGH coverage to make the eval meaningful
- ~500 chapters held out per arm? Affects scale of train corpus

## What "load-bearing" means concretely

If by tomorrow morning we can answer:

- **Yes**, the ontology stage produces a measurably different corpus character (already shown: 88.5% vs 57.4% ontology-vocab column alignment; 30% vs 80% hit@1 topic correspondence)
- **Yes**, the corpus differential is *the kind of difference* that should affect a downstream table-aware model (qualitatively yes; quantitative confirmation needs scale)
- **Pending**, whether this differential closes the gap to TAPEX/TableFormer on relational benchmarks (needs the v0.3 full release)

…then v0.3 is on track. The ontology is not ceremony — it's an axis we can choose to lean on or not, with measurable cost/benefit at the corpus and downstream-eval levels.

## Tomorrow morning's headline

If everything works:

> **The ontology is load-bearing for the kind of downstream task it was designed for** (slot-type prediction in 540-template space), but **trades raw FinePDFs-distribution alignment** for that grounding. The v0.2 SOTAB-CTA benchmark target may be the wrong eval — it's measuring against Schema.org's commerce/person vocab, not against the ontology our model is grounded in. Either re-target the eval or ship a corpus matched to SOTAB-CTA. The data supports both choices; we should pick deliberately, not by default.
